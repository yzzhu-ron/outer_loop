# Read-only review of the natural development experiment

Reviewed `full_paper/natural/protocol.json` and `experiment.py` frozen in `b827076`. No experiment code, protocol or output was changed. This review addresses the scientific interpretation, not a complete security or implementation audit.

The source/target boundary is clear in the inspected code: fitting uses source training utilities and source probe observations; onboarding accepts candidate metadata and a selected-ID observation callback; target task utilities enter afterward. The utility-rotation control preserves the equal-prior router because averaging the four permuted utility tensors is invariant. It therefore tests the observation-to-utility association without changing that starting policy. The changed hidden-backend assumption and the already-seen status of all three families are disclosed.

Four categorical worlds remove the binary shared-Gaussian total-order guarantee. They do not by themselves establish that the fitted channels are non-Blackwell-ordered, or that they differ for the supplied utilities. The subsequent [channel diagnostic](CHANNEL_DIAGNOSTICS.md) verifies all 28 channel pairs are incomparable in each of the three distinct fitted models, with numerical LP witnesses and exact rational separation bounds on row-normalized saved decimal probabilities. This establishes incomparability of the fitted channels, not retrieval-specific decision value or population-level incomparability. Four worlds also remain only two source corpus families with correlated backends per fold.

The **tempered** condition updates the realized posterior with likelihood^0.25. `branches` and `gains` still use the original likelihood and ordinary Bayes updates for acquisition planning. This is a valid prespecified update-tempering sensitivity, but it is not a coherent generative channel with correspondingly recomputed expected decision values. Tempering a likelihood also need not define a normalized alternative observation model. Preserve the frozen outputs and describe the actual intervention precisely.

`lookahead2` optimizes a two-step terminal utility increment per first-probe cost, with a second-step availability relaxation. The protocol discloses the finite-pool optimism. In addition, dividing by only the first cost is a heuristic acquisition score, not exact gain per total planned cost. Actual budget accounting and final costs still permit an honest empirical comparison. Any positive result would show this heuristic works, not an optimal two-step planning theorem.

The decision-entropy baseline uses the full four-bucket argmax vector as the world label. The saved-model diagnostic finds all four source worlds have distinct vectors in every standard fit. Label entropy therefore equals world entropy at every posterior, and the two acquisition objectives coincide exactly. These are duplicate baselines in this experiment, not independent evidence that the methods agree for deeper reasons.

The source likelihood pools all long-query probe buckets into family/action channels. The query router has four buckets, including short-query contexts with no matched probes. A posterior can therefore move short-query actions only through the global source-world association. This is extrapolation in the observation-to-utility bridge, and source utility rotation/tempering cannot certify that bridge's validity on target tasks.

All fixed policies and RRF are saved, but the baseline summary rows contain quality/count only. A final quality-cost argument needs their serving cost ledger as well as selector onboarding and serving costs. RRF's six searches per task matter: losing to RRF at quality alone does not rule out a total-cost tradeoff for a lower-cost router; nor does a count-matched probe budget prove such a tradeoff. The declared development gate can remain conservative while the report still shows this frontier.

## What to do with a failed development gate

Audit source-model policy capacity before extending acquisition. For each candidate four-bucket action vector, a shared posterior must satisfy all corresponding utility inequalities. Weak-tie feasibility is an upper relaxation; a purported attainable lower witness needs the real argmax tie convention, numerically verified posterior feasibility, and attained target utility. Target labels are permitted only in this evaluator bound. Its conclusion concerns this shared-posterior/five-action class. RRF lies outside that action menu, so an RRF comparison must retain that restriction explicitly.

The completed capacity audit places the mean optimistic posterior-router bound at 0.40766 nDCG, compared with 0.40891 for an unconstrained best-action-per-bucket oracle and 0.42118 for all-action RRF. Thus the four-bucket/single-action restriction explains more of this gap than shared-posterior coupling does. RRF exceeds even the bucket oracle in five of six environments. Arbitrary posterior witnesses need not be reachable by the allowed finite probe histories, so they are not evidence of a deployable acquisition procedure. An independent review recomputed all six deterministic lower witnesses and checked all 52 policies strictly above the saved per-environment upper bounds; all 52 were numerically infeasible. The audit now raises on solver failure instead of silently treating it as infeasibility. Retain its explicit floating-certificate caveat.

If the reachable class itself is weak, the next promising intervention changes the utility bridge or action menu. One practical candidate is to start from strong RRF and learn which subsets of its query actions preserve quality at lower total cost. Another is to learn context-conditional utility transport from probes that cover short and long queries, validated on held-out source families. Both require new empirical work; neither inherits novelty from a generic VOI calculation.

The timing/coverage results in THEORY.md can then guide a controlled experiment: freeze the utilities, observation model and context pool; change only workload weights; test whether source-predicted acquisition rankings reverse as predicted. That experiment would reveal whether the model understands decision-relevant context coverage. It cannot establish transfer while the real-data model remains dominated by simple baselines or while all evaluated corpora have already informed development.
