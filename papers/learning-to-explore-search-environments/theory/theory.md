# Which probes can change a search decision?

**Working theory note · 9 September 2026.** The constructive objective is to reduce uncertainty about useful search actions. Probe difficulty, successful document rediscovery, and knowledge of the environment are different objectives. The first pilot motivates this distinction; it does not establish that the full search transcript is uninformative. The results below are finite-model statements and exact constructions, not new retrieval measurements or a novelty claim.

**Setting.** Let Θ be a finite set of possible environments and A a finite menu. An action can be a complete query-conditioned router, with Uθ(a) ∈ [0,1] its expected quality on future tasks. Write Vθ = maxₐ Uθ(a), and ℓθ(p) = Vθ − ΣₐpₐUθ(a) for a randomized router p. The oracle is the best member of this menu; it is a per-query oracle only if the menu contains every relevant query-to-action mapping. Future query observations are already included in a router's definition. All environments share the same known task-context distribution in the indistinguishability statements; otherwise the query itself can reveal the environment and must be included in the observation law.

Onboarding produces transcript H from adaptively chosen probes and their observations. H includes *all* allowed information: sampled documents, probe text, ranks, search results, public API documentation, and internal random choices when needed. Source training is conditioned on in advance. Environments that differ in disclosed documentation are already distinguishable. Neither a synthetic hit score nor a compact profile should silently replace H in an impossibility claim.

**1. Exact residual uncertainty is a decision radius.** For a nonempty transcript-compatible set S ⊆ Θ, define

$$R(S)=\min_{p\in\Delta(A)}\max_{\theta\in S}\ell_\theta(p)=\max_{w\in\Delta(S)}\left[\sum_\theta w_\theta V_\theta-\max_a\sum_\theta w_\theta U_\theta(a)\right].$$

The equality follows from finite minimax duality applied to the loss matrix Lθa = Vθ − Uθ(a). The right side is the largest Bayes regret over priors supported on S, or worst-prior expected value of perfect information. A dual optimum needs at most |A| environments: a basic optimum of the dual LP has at most |A| positive prior weights. Thus a small group of conflicting environments can certify irreducible regret even when Θ is large.

R(S) = 0 exactly when all environments in S share an optimal action. For the forward direction, zero nonnegative expected loss forces every action in the support of an optimal p to be optimal in every environment. The reverse direction chooses the common optimum. Also R(S′) ≤ R(S) for S′ ⊆ S. These are decision statements: recovering every environmental detail is unnecessary.

For a fixed *deterministic noiseless* adaptive probe tree, leaves partition Θ into sets Sₕ. Its optimal worst-case downstream regret is exactly maxₕ R(Sₕ): choose the minimax router independently at each leaf. The best next robust probe minimizes max_z R(S ∩ {θ : observation(θ,q) = z}), over outcomes with nonempty compatible sets. With noisy probes, literal positive-likelihood support often never shrinks; use posterior risk or confidence sets instead. A nonempty confidence set satisfying Pθ(θ ∉ S_H) ≤ δ gives Eθℓθ(p_H) ≤ EθR(S_H) + δ for its minimax router, because regret is at most one outside the set. If a procedure can produce an empty set, specify a fallback router and charge that event to the failure probability; an empty source model is not a zero-regret certificate. Coverage is an assumption to establish, not an automatic consequence of a source ensemble.

**Pairwise disagreement misses multiway conflicts.** Define D(S) = maxθ,θ′∈S minₚ[ℓθ(p)+ℓθ′(p)]. Then D(S)/2 ≤ R(S), because any p pays at least that sum on the worst pair. There is no upper bound on R from D alone that vanishes at D = 0. The three utility rows (0,1,1), (1,0,1), (1,1,0) have pairwise common optima, so D = 0; yet R = 1/3. Uniform action mixing attains it, and the uniform environment prior certifies it. This is not dependent on ties: rows (0,1,1−ε), (1−ε,0,1), (1,1−ε,0) have unique winners and give D = ε while R = (1+ε)/3 for 0 < ε ≤ 1. Symmetry again certifies both values. A probe diagnostic should therefore retain the utility matrix, not only count reversed winning pairs.

For two actions, an exact formula is available. Put gθ = Uθ(1) − Uθ(0), α = maxθ(gθ)₊, β = maxθ(−gθ)₊. Then R = αβ/(α+β), taking zero if α+β = 0. An optimal probability of action 1 is α/(α+β). Proof: the worst regret is max{α(1−p), βp}; equalize its two branches. This also shows why a rare but severe wrong action can matter more than the number of environments voting for it.

**2. Adaptive exploration needs an observation-to-decision link.** If two environments have identical observation kernels for every allowed probe after every reachable history, induction gives identical transcript laws under every adaptive strategy. Their final action distributions are therefore identical. If this holds for every environment in S, no amount of these probes beats R(S), and the minimax mixture attains it. Perfect rediscovery is compatible with R = 1/2: let every probe return the same known-document success while the two utility rows are (1,0) and (0,1).

More generally let P and Q be the complete transcript laws in two environments, and Dθθ′ = minₚ[ℓθ(p)+ℓθ′(p)]. Every method satisfies

$$\max\{\mathbb E_P\ell_\theta(p_H),\mathbb E_Q\ell_{\theta'}(p_H)\}\ \geq\ \frac{D_{\theta\theta'}}2\bigl(1-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\bigr).$$

Proof: integrate the pointwise sum-loss bound against the common part min(dP,dQ), whose mass is 1−TV; nonnegative losses make the remaining terms harmless. If each conditional probe KL divergence is at most κ and at most B probes are allowed, the adaptive KL chain rule and Pinsker give the weaker bound Dθθ′/2 · max{0, 1−√(Bκ/2)}. All side information must be included before asserting this bound. This is a standard two-point lower-bound argument, specialized to *action incompatibility* rather than environment-classification error.

There is a direct task-distribution issue: the same corpus and backend can serve different future workloads. Corpus observations alone cannot reveal a workload parameter that is independent of them. Task-type documentation, an agreed task mix, or a source-to-target relationship must supply that link. A theorem about an assumed source family is not a distribution-free guarantee for new corpora.

**3. A sufficient alignment condition gives a usable error bound.** Consider a structural model on an admissible parameter domain T ⊆ ℝᵈ with nonempty interior, n scalar probe means z = Vθ, and action contrasts relative to a reference router g = Wθ+b. The domain must respect the bounded probe means and utilities; nonconstant linear means cannot stay bounded on all of ℝᵈ. Include every action in g, with the reference action's contrast identically zero. Contrasts are identifiable from noiseless z throughout T exactly when ker(V) ⊆ ker(W), equivalently W = CV for some matrix C. Sufficiency is substitution. For necessity, any v with Vv = 0 but Wv ≠ 0 allows two sufficiently close points θ and θ+tv in an interior ball of T with the same observations and different contrasts. On a lower-dimensional domain, the kernel condition is sufficient but need not be necessary. Recovering all contrasts is stronger than recovering the winning action: a varying contrast can remain on the same side of every decision boundary.

Allow an approximate target relationship ‖g − (Cz+b)‖∞ ≤ ε_align. Fix the n probe coordinates and the reconstruction coefficients C,b before collecting the observations used in this bound; source learning may be conditioned on in advance. Suppose each probe mean is estimated from r independent [0,1]-valued observations with that mean. Independence between different probe coordinates is unnecessary for the union bound. The router selects argmaxₐ(Cẑ+b)ₐ over all actions, including the reference action with an estimated contrast fixed at zero. For 0 < δ < 1, a union of Hoeffding bounds and the argmax inequality give, with probability at least 1−δ,

$$\ell_\theta(\hat a)\leq\min\left\{1,\ 2\varepsilon_{\rm align}+2\lVert C\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2n/\delta)}{2r}}\right\}.$$

Here ‖C‖∞→∞ is the largest absolute row sum. Proof: the maximum mean-estimation error is at most the square-root term; multiply by C and add ε_align to bound every contrast error. Selecting the largest estimated contrast loses at most twice this error. Omitting the reference candidate breaks the statement: a perfectly known negative contrast must not force selection of an inferior alternative. The bound separates *unaligned probes* (ε_align), *unstable reconstruction* (large ‖C‖), and sampling noise. More repetitions cannot repair the first term. Learning C and bounding ε_align requires source labels and transfer assumptions; the target's rediscovery outcomes alone do not certify either one. Adaptively choosing coordinates, stopping sample collection, or fitting C from the same noise requires an additional uniform or sequential concentration argument. A [−1,1]-valued paired contrast needs the corresponding larger concentration constant.

**4. Three acquisition rules to test.** The rules below require a source-trained model of downstream utilities and probe outcomes. Candidate generation, costs, observation fields, and final routers must be identical across selectors. Target task labels do not enter any acquisition calculation.

1. **Bayesian decision value per cost.** For a current environment prior/posterior w, let R_w = E_w Vθ − maxₐE_w Uθ(a). Select q maximizing [R_w − E_z R_{w(·|z,q)}]/c_q. The numerator equals E_z maxₐE[Uθ(a)|z,q] − maxₐE_w Uθ(a), so it values observations that change a useful action. It is nonnegative by convexity of max. This is ordinary value of information; the research work is learning a calibrated search-specific model that transfers.

2. **Robust decision-radius reduction.** For noiseless finite models, maximize [R(S) − max_z R(S_z)]/c_q, retaining the multiway LP certificate. For noisy empirical models, substitute a coverage-calibrated uncertainty set or optimize expected posterior regret; removing every world with an unlikely observation without a coverage argument is unsafe. This criterion can be conservative when the source family contains an implausible adversarial world.

3. **Projected contrast variance.** In a linear Gaussian surrogate, a probe y = v_qᵀθ+ε, prior covariance Σ, and independent noise variance σ_q² reduce the variance of a downstream contrast wᵀθ by (wᵀΣv_q)²/(σ_q²+v_qᵀΣv_q). Sum over source task bins and competing action pairs, using prechosen task-mix weights and optionally larger weights near predicted decision boundaries, then divide by cost. The identity follows by multiplying the Gaussian rank-one covariance update by w on both sides. The variance calculation is exact under the surrogate; it is **not** an exact regret reduction or a general acquisition guarantee. Freeze weights while scoring candidates.

A source-world ensemble can implement rule 1 immediately: store utility vectors from labeled source tasks; fit likelihoods for probe-family outcomes; update world weights from target probes; score the expected change in the winning utility vector. Leave out entire source corpus families when validating the model, and compare with generic environment information gain. If only two source corpora exist, treat the result as a debugging experiment. Conditional utilities should use a documented task-mix prior, not hidden target query frequencies.

**5. Exact counterexamples and a stopping trap.** [verified_results.json](verified_results.json) uses exact rational calculations for regret, radius, and probability values; entropy values are floating-point evaluations of the stated finite distributions. There is no simulation noise. In a 16-world model, one latent bit decides the best action and three independent bits are nuisance. Initial Bayes regret is 1/2. A hard independent probe has rediscovery 1/2 and no decision value. A nuisance probe reveals three environment bits, with no decision value. An easier aligned probe has rediscovery 9/10 and only 0.108 bits of environment information, but reduces expected regret to 2/5. Thus both “choose the hardest” and full-environment information gain select a useless probe; uniform random selection obtains regret 7/15, versus 2/5 for decision value. The nuisance probe also records an independent Bernoulli(19/20) rediscovery hit; the full joint observation is included in the calculation. These are deliberately constructed examples, not realistic estimates of search performance.

The greedy rule also has a failure mode worth testing. If the optimal action is the XOR of two independent hidden bits and separate probes reveal each bit, each probe's one-step decision value is zero, but the pair's value is 1/2. Decision value is not generally adaptively submodular. Do not conclude that exploration is exhausted merely because every one-step estimate is zero. Compare small two-probe lookahead or fixed diverse batches. Under an explicit quality-versus-cost coefficient λ and N future tasks, a one-step probe is locally worthwhile when N·VOI(q) > λc_q; this is not a globally optimal stopping rule in the presence of complementary probes.

**What this adds to the research plan.** Measure whether source-trained probe models reduce *held-out downstream decision radius/regret*, including multiway conflicts, before optimizing rediscovery difficulty. Report the reconstruction/alignment error separately from sampling uncertainty. Ablate probe sets with matched difficulty but different action-contrast predictiveness. Treat high oracle headroom, identifiable decision differences, transferable calibration, and affordable exploration as four separate questions; failure of one query/probe library does not settle the others.

The building blocks here are established: minimax statistical decision theory and LP duality; Le Cam/Pinsker lower bounds; value of information and Bayesian experimental design; observability ideas also central to partial monitoring; and Gaussian optimal-design updates. Useful starting references are [Bandit Algorithms](https://tor-lattimore.com/downloads/book/book.pdf) and the proposal's [DREAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.02790) comparison. We are not claiming these results as new theorems. A defensible contribution would be a search-specific observable-decision model, useful diagnostics and acquisition methods, and evidence that its action-relevant uncertainty predicts real adaptation. That novelty assessment still needs a targeted literature comparison and empirical transfer results.

**Reproduce.** From the repository root, run `python papers/learning-to-explore-search-environments/theory/verify_theory.py`, then `python -m unittest discover -s papers/learning-to-explore-search-environments/theory -v`. All calculations use the Python standard library. Tests include exact primal/dual agreement on 60 seeded small games, asymmetric and degenerate two-action cases, three-way certificates with and without ties, noise and zero-probability outcomes, complementarity, covariance validity, square-system shape validation, and reference-action selection. A bounded interior-domain construction demonstrates nonidentifiability without leaving the admissible parameter set. Vertex enumeration is deliberately limited to small finite models; a production implementation should use an LP solver.
