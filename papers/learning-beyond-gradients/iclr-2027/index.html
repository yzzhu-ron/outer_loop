The Outer Loop: A Unified Theory of Gradient-Free LLM Improvement Loops

A formal theory of the outer loop — the pattern shared by every frozen-weight LLM self-improvement system: prior mass, feedback information, evaluation noise, deceptive landscapes, and a composed certified-improvement theorem, in one model.

Abstract

What this paper is about

A rapidly growing family of systems improves at tasks without touching model weights: an outer loop proposes edits to a textual or programmatic artifact — a prompt, a skill document, an agent harness, a training script — scores each candidate with a noisy evaluator, and keeps what works, with a frozen large language model as the proposal distribution.

We give a single formal model containing these systems and prove that its behavior is governed by a few measurable resources: the prior mass the proposer places on good artifacts (controls hitting time Levin-style; boost γ is estimable from samples); the information content of feedback (scalar rewards can be exponentially slower than structured traces); the noise–gap ratio of the evaluator (a single-evaluation greedy ratchet fed pure noise reports a realized median "improvement" of 3.4σ after 2,000 null edits); the deceptiveness of the landscape (archives are exponentially faster than tolerant greedy search); and the description length of the artifact (an Occam bound predicts both context collapse and validation overfitting).

Beyond per-resource analyses, we prove a composed certified-improvement theorem (Theorem 6.1): a gated loop finds and soundly certifies a near-optimal artifact in O((σ²/Δ²) ln(B/δ) ln(1/δ) / (γ q₀(Gε))) evaluator calls. We map each result to documented behaviors of deployed systems and state falsifiable scaling predictions with a pre-registered measurement protocol.

Contributions

Seven results in one model

§3 · Prior Mass

Hitting time is Levin-style

Expected proposals to an ε-good artifact equal the inverse of the proposer's prior mass on the good set. Trace-conditioning gives a multiplicative boost γ — and γ is estimable at finite cost (Prop. 3.5).

§4 · Feedback Information

Scalar vs. structured traces

Binary 0/1 reward requires 2L queries to identify an L-bit target. First-error traces need only L/2. Distance feedback needs L+1. Exponential separation (Thm. 4.1, 4.2).

§5 · Ratchet Illusion

3.4σ from pure noise

A greedy ratchet on null (zero-value) edits accumulates 3.4σ median "improvement" at t=2000. Upper bound: σ√(2 ln t) (Thm. 5.1). Gated ratchet with N=85 repeats certifies a whole run (Cor. 5.3).

§5 · Deceptive Landscapes

Archives beat greedy exponentially

Tolerant greedy search on a k-wide deceptive valley takes Ω(ε−k) steps. Archive search takes only O(k²) steps to the global optimum (Thm. 5.4).

§6 · Composition

Certified improvement theorem

Prior mass × noise-gap × gated selection in one composed statement (Thm. 6.1). False-reject slowdown explicit as (1−δ/B). Proof in Appendix E.

§7 · Occam Bound

U-shaped risk predicts overfitting

Description length of the artifact enters an Occam generalization bound. Predicts both context collapse (too short) and validation overfitting (too long), matching observed failures (Thm. 7.1, Cor. 7.2).

§8 · Goodhart Limits

In-model reward is bounded

When the evaluator is itself an LLM, any bounded reward function's range is filled by some artifact (Prop. 8.1). The model cannot distinguish its own optimization target from all other behaviors.

Theorem 6.1 · Budget Calculator

How many evaluator calls do you need?

Theorem 6.1 gives the total evaluator call budget for a gated loop to find and soundly certify a near-optimal artifact:

Theorem 6.1 — Composed Certified Improvement
A gated artifact-search loop certifies a Δ-improvement over the incumbent (with failure probability ≤ δ, across a budget of B candidates) using at most

C = (8σ²/Δ²) · ln(B/δ) · ln(1/δ) / (γ · q₀(Gε))

evaluator calls in expectation, where γ is the trace-conditioning boost, q₀(Gε) is the proposer's prior mass on the ε-good set, N = ⌈(8σ²/Δ²)·ln(B/δ)⌉ repeats per gate.
