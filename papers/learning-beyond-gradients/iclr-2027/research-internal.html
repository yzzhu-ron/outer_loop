Chosen title (tentative)
The Outer Loop: A Unified Theory of Gradient-Free LLM Improvement Loops

Documented alternate: "The Ratchet Illusion: Resources, Certificates, and Limits of LLM-Guided Artifact Search" — held in reserve as the provocative/citation-shorthand variant.

Rationale

"The Outer Loop" coins the category name — the pattern every frozen-weight self-improvement system runs. Short, memorable, natural citation handle. Does not duplicate the Weng (2026) blog title. The competitor was "The Ratchet Illusion" as lead phrase — stronger hook, but risks narrowing the paper's perceived scope to just §5. Decision: keep "ratchet illusion" as a concept name within the paper; use "The Outer Loop" as submission title.

Paper Ideas

Speculative threads. Organized by what's being optimized, how, and what theory angle.

The current paper analyzes the outer loop in the abstract. These ideas explore it concretely — specific instantiations, new theory directions, and empirical programs.

A · What to optimize — the artifact space

The paper treats 𝒜 as an abstract countable space. A follow-on empirical program could systematically vary what kind of artifact is being searched.

Empirical · Web agents
Harness Search for Web Agents hot

The ShopGym / CUA setting: freeze the agent model, run the outer loop over the harness — memory policy, retry logic, observation preprocessing, tool-call ordering, context truncation strategy. What is the artifact space? How does γ vary across harness components?

Connects directly to: Meta-Harness (Lee et al. 2026), Appendix I of the theory paper, and the ShopGym harness-track proposal.

Key question: which harness components have the highest γ? Which are deceptive (wide valley between local and global opt)?
Empirical · Code / ML research
Artifact Hierarchy: Prompt → Skill → Harness → Training Config warm

A hierarchy of artifact types with increasing description length ℓ(a) and decreasing proposer prior mass q₀. The Occam U-shape (§7) predicts an optimal level. Can we measure the γ and q₀ at each level empirically?

Level 1: system prompt. Level 2: skill document (few-shot + CoT template). Level 3: harness code. Level 4: training config (like Karpathy's autoresearch). Each level is a separate paper; together they form a systematic empirical program.

Key question: does γ increase or decrease as you go up the hierarchy?
Theory + Empirical
Compositional Artifact Search speculative

Instead of treating the artifact as monolithic, decompose it: a = (a₁, a₂, …, aₖ) where each component is searched semi-independently. E.g., a prompt = system + few-shot + CoT template. Does compositionality help? The Levin bound on q₀ for the joint space vs. the product of marginals.

Key question: is the joint prior mass larger or smaller than the product of marginals? (Depends on correlation structure.)

B · How to optimize — the selector and loop architecture

Theory
Multi-Objective Outer Loops: Pareto Archives and the Archive Rate warm

The current paper treats the selector as single-objective. But deployed systems (Meta-Harness, ShopGym) have multiple objectives: speed vs. accuracy vs. cost vs. robustness. Extend the archive analysis (Thm 5.4) to Pareto fronts. What is the analogue of the hitting-time bound when the target is a Pareto-optimal region rather than a point?

Key question: does the exponential advantage of archives over greedy persist in the multi-objective case?
Empirical
Selector Ablation Study: Ratchet vs. Archive vs. Beam vs. Pareto hot

The theory predicts archive > greedy on deceptive landscapes and greedy ≈ archive on non-deceptive ones. Can we verify this experimentally by varying the selector on a fixed task and proposer? The deception width k could be operationalized as: how many "worse" intermediate steps must you accept to reach the global optimum.

Pre-registered: fix proposer = GPT-4o, task = HumanEval-Hard, vary sel ∈ {single-eval ratchet, gated ratchet (N=85), archive, Pareto archive}.
Theory + Systems
Asynchronous Outer Loops: Multiple Proposers in Parallel speculative

Current theory assumes a single sequential proposer. Real systems (GEPA population, AlphaEvolve island model) run multiple proposers in parallel. Does parallelism help beyond the trivial γ-scaling? Are there interference effects (proposers stepping on each other's accepted artifacts)?

Key question: does the gated ratchet budget scale linearly with the number of parallel proposers?

C · Around the frozen weights — harness design space

The outer loop keeps weights frozen but varies what wraps them. This is a rich design space that no paper has fully mapped.

Systems + Empirical
A Taxonomy of Harness Components and Their Searchability hot

Map out the full design space of what a harness can vary:

Memory: sliding window / summary / episodic / external retrieval
Observation: raw HTML / accessibility tree / screenshot / diff / structured extraction
Action space: click+type / high-level API / code execution / hybrid
Retry logic: immediate / backoff / reflective / rollback
Context: compression ratio / what gets included / ordering

For each dimension: measure γ (how much traces help proposer improvement), q₀ (baseline proposer mass), and landscape topology (deceptive or not). This is the empirical program that makes the theory actionable for practitioners.

Key output: a "searchability score" per harness component — how much can the outer loop improve it?
Theory
The γ Measurement Paper — Pre-Registered Empirical Protocol hot

Appendix I of the theory paper has the pre-registered protocol (120 buggy programs, 70 patches/condition, Wilson intervals). Execute this. The output is a measurement of γ for a specific proposer (Claude 3.5 Sonnet or GPT-4o) on a specific task class. Then vary: model size, task type, feedback modality (scalar/trace/distance). This is a focused 6-page empirical paper with strong theoretical grounding.

Pre-registered protocol already written: iclr-2027/main.tex Appendix I. Just needs execution (~1 GPU-day).
Theory + Empirical · Web agents
Harness-Search for ShopGym: Outer Loop Applied to E-Commerce CUA hot

The most direct application of the theory to the ShopGym paper. Use the outer loop to search for the optimal CUA harness on e-commerce web tasks. Key contributions: (1) apply gated ratchet to avoid ratchet illusion in harness selection; (2) measure γ for harness proposals from traces; (3) show archive search beats greedy on deceptive harness landscapes (tasks with local-optima shortcuts that fail at checkout).

Connects: ShopGym harness-track proposal + theory Thm 6.1.

D · New theory directions

Theory
Self-Referential Outer Loops: When the Artifact Includes the Loop seed

Appendix J of the current paper states the open problem: what happens when the artifact being optimized includes the optimization algorithm itself (Darwin Gödel Machine, ADAS)? The theory breaks because the proposer distribution changes as the artifact changes. Is there a fixed-point characterization? When does self-reference help vs. destabilize?

Related: Gödel machines (Schmidhuber), ADAS (Hu et al. 2025). The theory has no tools for this yet.
Theory
Information-Theoretic Lower Bounds: Are the Thm 6.1 Rates Tight? speculative

Theorem 6.1 gives an upper bound of O((σ²/Δ²) ln(B/δ) ln(1/δ) / (γ q₀)). Is this tight? A matching lower bound would show the gated ratchet is information-theoretically optimal among all artifact-search strategies with the same γ and q₀. The ratchet lower bound (Thm 5.1) is already tight; does it compose?

Theory + Empirical
Feedback Engineering: Designing the Evaluator to Maximize γ warm

The feedback-information result (§4) shows traces beat scalar rewards exponentially. But what is the optimal feedback format for a given task? Can you design the evaluator output to maximize the proposer's conditioning boost γ? This inverts the usual direction: instead of analyzing given feedback, design feedback for maximum γ. Relevant for practitioners who control the evaluator.

Key question: given task T and proposer LLM, what feedback format maximizes γ?
Theory
Transfer and Warm-Starting: γ Across Task Distributions seed

γ measures how much traces from task T help the proposer on task T. But what about traces from a related task T′? If you run the outer loop on T′ first and collect traces, does γ on T improve? This is the theory of transfer learning for the outer loop — connecting to in-context learning generalization.

Prioritization guide

hot = directly actionable, pre-conditions met, ~1 GPU-day
warm = needs scoping, 1-2 months
cool = interesting but 6+ months
seed = open problem, no clear method yet

Blog Ideas

Companion posts, explainers, and accessible takes. Not all need to be published — some are just good for crystallizing thinking.

The companion blog covered the lit-review angle. These ideas go further — extracting the most striking results into standalone readable pieces, or taking provocative angles that the paper can't.

High-priority — core results deserve their own posts

01
The 3.4σ Illusion: How Every LLM Optimizer Is Fooling You Right Now hot
Lead with the demo: drag the slider, watch pure noise produce 3.4σ of "improvement." Then explain why every prompt optimizer, harness tuner, and self-improvement loop you've heard of is doing exactly this — and how to fix it with N=85 repeats.
Audience: ML practitioners · Format: interactive (embed the demo from index.html) · Tie-in: links to paper §5 + interactive demo
02
Why Your Traces Are Worth 2L Times More Than Your Scores hot
The feedback-channel result in one story: if you're only giving your optimizer pass/fail, you're leaving exponential speedup on the table. Walk through the L-bit identification game with a concrete coding analogy. Explain why GEPA's 35× speedup isn't a surprise — it's the theory.
Audience: MLEs building eval pipelines · Tie-in: §4, Thm 4.2, feedback demo
03
The Outer Loop: One Pattern to Rule Them All hot
The accessibility piece. Show that GEPA, Meta-Harness, Karpathy's autoresearch, Heuristic Learning, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, and SkillOpt are all running the same loop — different vocabulary, same tuple (𝒜, F, q, sel, M). Draw the unifying diagram. Non-technical version of the paper's §1–2.
Audience: broad ML, researchers new to this area · Format: explanatory, heavily diagrammed · Companion to the paper
04
Archives Beat Greedy — A Visual Proof warm
Interactive valley animation: show the greedy optimizer getting stuck, the archive escaping. Explain why every successfully deployed evolutionary system independently invented archives (GEPA populations, AlphaEvolve island models, Meta-Harness version pools). The math is Thm 5.4 but told visually.
Audience: ML practitioners · Format: interactive, animated · Tie-in: deception demo, §5

Medium-priority — good for positioning and community-building

05
What GEPA, Karpathy, and Heuristic Learning Have in Common (And What None of Them Knew)
A "did you know these papers were all doing the same thing?" narrative. Three independent systems, three different vocabularies, one formal model. Likely to be shared by the communities around each paper — good for cross-pollination.
Audience: researchers · Format: essay · Good for arXiv announcement
06
How to Actually Know If Your LLM Improved: A Field Guide
Practical guide derived from the theory. How many evaluation repeats do you need (N formula)? When is a scalar reward enough vs. when do you need traces? When should you use an archive? Reads like an engineering checklist — no math required.
Audience: ML engineers · Format: actionable checklist with formulas · Downloadable PDF version
07
Goodhart Meets Frozen Weights: Why LLM Judges Can't Be Fully Trusted
Prop 8.1 made accessible: any bounded LLM evaluator can be gamed — there's always a prompt that maxes it out without doing the real task. Concrete examples: jailbreaks, prompt hacking, reward hacking in RL. Explains why you always need a held-out human evaluation at the end.
Audience: ML safety, practitioners · Tie-in: §8
08
The γ Factor: The Number That Predicts Whether Your Self-Improvement Loop Will Work
Explains γ (trace-conditioning boost) to a non-technical audience. What it means, how to estimate it from data, and what typical values look like. Positioned as "the one number your team should measure before running any outer-loop experiment." Could be the launch piece for the γ measurement paper.
Audience: ML teams building self-improving systems · Tie-in: Prop 3.5, Appendix I, γ measurement paper

Longer-form / exploratory

09
From Prompts to Harnesses: A Map of What You Can Optimize Around a Frozen LLM
The hierarchy of artifacts — prompt → skill document → harness code → training config — with estimated γ and q₀ at each level. Synthesizes the theory with the ShopGym / Meta-Harness empirical work. Could serve as the intro section of the harness taxonomy paper.
Format: long explainer, 2000–3000 words · Potential basis for harness taxonomy paper §1
10
The Evaluation Crisis in Self-Improving AI: A Decade in Review
Connects our ratchet illusion result to the long history of evaluation failures in ML: Henderson et al. 2018 (RL reproducibility), Dodge et al. 2019 (NLP variance), Agarwal et al. 2021 (deep RL), and now LLM self-improvement loops. The same problem keeps recurring. Our gated ratchet is the first certified fix.
Audience: ML researchers, broader AI community · Format: historical essay · Citation-heavy, good for reaching evaluation-statistics community
11
Can an AI Improve Itself? A Precise Answer
The public-facing version of the whole paper. Answers the question precisely: yes, under conditions given by Thm 6.1; no, when Goodhart's Law (§8) applies; and "it looks like yes but it's an illusion" (§5). Avoids hype in both directions. Could be a magazine-style piece.
Audience: general tech-literate · Format: 1500-word essay, no equations · arXiv + social media launch piece
