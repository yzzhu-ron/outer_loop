What the paper claims, and what survives scrutiny

The paper defines an artifact-search system (Def. 2.1) — frozen-LLM proposer, noisy evaluator, selection rule, memory — and proves six elementary-but-sharp results: hitting time = inverse prior mass (Thm 3.2), exponential scalar-vs-trace feedback separation (Thms 4.1–4.2), the ratchet illusion under noise with a log-cost repair (Thm 5.1, Prop 5.2, Cor 5.3), greedy-vs-archive separation on deceptive landscapes (Thm 5.4), an Occam bound with a U-shaped risk in artifact length (Thm 6.1, Cor 6.2), and Goodhart transfer limits (Prop 7.1).

Bottom line of my verification: the mathematical core is sound. All six proof groups check out as arguments. What fails is statement-level hygiene: one theorem's construction contradicts its own statement (Thm 5.4's "unique optimum" isn't unique), one stated constant is stronger than what the proof yields (Prop 5.2 converse), one construction exits the paper's own model (Prop 7.1(iii) needs F ∉ [0,1]), and three prose numbers are contradicted by the paper's own committed code — including a "zero false accepts" claim that the paper's own theory predicts should be ≈1.6, and that the committed seed in fact realizes as 2.

That last point deserves emphasis because it is the paper's moral aimed at itself: a paper whose thesis is "single noisy evaluations manufacture fictitious certainty" reported a single lucky-looking zero as if it were a property. Across 20 fresh seeds I measured 0–3 false accepts (mean 1.55, matching the Poisson prediction 1.61). The fix makes the paper stronger: the gate's 0.5%-per-run false-accept rate sits comfortably inside the δ = 5% certificate, which is exactly what Cor 5.3 promises — no more, no less.

Everything mandatory is fixable in days. The structural criticism that should drive the rebuttal is Reviewer 1's W1/Q2 — no theorem requires the unified object — and I sketch a composed theorem (R9) that would answer it with machinery already in the paper.

Findings

E1–E4 independently confirm Reviewer 1 (W3–W6). N1–N4 are new to this review.

E1 confirms W3 · Thm 5.4's landscape contradicts its own statement

With f(i) = k−i on [0,k] and f(i) = i−k on [k,2k]: f(0) = f(2k) = k. The "unique global optimum 2k" is not unique — strict greedy "failing" at 0 is resting at a global maximizer. The hitting-time separations survive (they concern reaching 2k), but the narrative collapses as stated. Also: the statement says proposals go "to a uniformly random neighbor" while proof and code use the lazy boundary convention (h₀ = 2/ε, not 1/ε).

E2 confirms W5 · Prop 5.2's converse claims more than its proof delivers

Bretagnolle–Huber: 2δ ≥ ½e⁻ᴬᴪ ⇒ N ≥ (2σ²/Δ²)·ln(1/(4δ)). The statement asserts ln(1/(2δ)) > ln(1/(4δ)) — i.e. the stated lower bound is stronger than the proven one. "Up to the stated constant" in a proof does not license strengthening a statement.

E3 confirms W4 · Prop 7.1(iii) exits the model

Def 2.1 fixes F : A → [0,1]; the construction takes F(aⱼ) = −j → −∞ ("after affine rescaling" — no affine map unbounds a bounded codomain). The point survives in bounded form; restate it inside the model.

E4 confirms W6 · Three prose numbers contradicted by the committed code

(a) "≈3.9σ" is the bound; the realized median is 3.39σ. (b) "≈7.6 accepts" should be H₂₀₀₀ ≈ 8.2. (c) "accepted zero null edits across all runs" — the committed seed yields 2; theory predicts ≈1.6; 20 fresh seeds give 0–3. The zero was a one-seed illusion, in a paper about one-evaluation illusions.

N1 new · Thm 5.1(ii): the stated rule and the counted statistic disagree

The rule is "accept iff the measured score beats the incumbent's measured score" — which implies a measured baseline ŝ₀ exists. The proof and simulation instead count records of the edit stream alone, so the first edit is auto-accepted. With a measured incumbent, expected accepts = Hₜ₊₁ − 1 ≈ 7.18 at t=2000, not Hₜ = 8.18 — a full unit. Pick one convention and state it; the theorem is correct under "empty incumbent", which is what the code implements.

N2 new · §3 overclaims what Prop 3.3 proves about γ < 1

§3 says misleading feedback (γ < 1) "provably slows search below cold sampling (Proposition 3.3)". Prop 3.3 gives E[T] ≥ 1/(2q̄) − 1; with q̄ = γq₀ this exceeds the cold rate 1/q₀ only when γ < ≈½. For γ ∈ [½, 1) the cited proposition proves nothing of the sort. Cheap fix: if the conditional good-mass equals γq₀ at every step, the geometric argument gives E[T] = 1/(γq₀) exactly > 1/q₀ — state that version, or weaken the prose.

N3 new (sharpens R1's pass) · Headline N = 85 is not "the Corollary 5.3 prescription"

Main text §8 and Fig 2's caption attribute N = 85 to Cor 5.3, whose constant (via Prop 5.2) is 16 — giving N = 170. The 85 comes from a one-sided constant 8 disclosed only in App G, with an extra assumption (known incumbent mean) that contradicts the ratchet model two paragraphs earlier. Either state a one-sided corollary explicitly or run with N = 170; do not let the main text cite a constant the cited result doesn't contain.

N4 new · Fig 3 legend mixes worst-case bound and average-case law

The green dashed curve plotted is the average-case law L/2+1, but the legend labels it "first-error feedback: ≤ L (Thm 4.2)" — a worst-case statement. Markers agreeing with a curve labeled by a theorem they don't instantiate invites misreading (compounding W7's plotting-theory-against-itself issue: the distance arm is the constant L+1 by construction and the scalar arm samples the exact law rather than searching).

Revision plan

Ordered by priority. R1–R4 are conditions for acceptance; R5–R8 should be done for camera-ready; R9–R11 are where the score moves.

R1 must · Fix Thm 5.4's landscape and proposal convention

Drop-in statement: asymmetric ascent makes 2k strictly optimal and leaves every recurrence untouched (valley: p=ε/2, q=½; ascent: p=½, q=ε/2).

f(i) = k - i           on [0, k]
f(i) = 2(i - k)        on [k, 2k]   % f(2k) = 2k > k = f(0): unique optimum

And in the statement: "each step proposes a uniformly random element of {i−1, i+1}; out-of-range proposals are rejected (the walk stays)" — this is what the proof's h₀ = 2/ε and the code already assume.

R2 must · Correct Prop 5.2's converse constant

... requires N ≥ (2σ²/Δ²) ln(1/(4δ)).

R3 must · Restate Prop 7.1(iii) inside F : A → [0,1]

Bounded version, same moral: take F(aⱼ) = 2⁻ⱼ ↓ 0 and b(aⱼ) = 1 − 2⁻ⱼ⁻¹ so F̃(aⱼ) ↑ 1. A proxy ratchet accepts every step; measured progress → the full range while true score → its infimum. Conclusion to state: "no transfer inequality F-progress ≥ ψ(F̃-progress) with ψ bounded below on positive arguments can hold" — range-filling divergence rather than ±∞.

R4 must · Fix the three prose numbers — and use (c) as a feature

(a) §8: "manufacturing a ≈3.4σ 'improvement' (median 3.39σ; the asymptotic ceiling is σ√(2 ln t) = 3.90σ)". (b) §5.1: "accept ≈8.2 null edits (H₂₀₀₀)". (c) App G: "2 false accepts across 8×10⁵ gated decisions (per-run rate 0.5%, within the δ = 5% certificate; the theory's own Poisson prediction is ≈1.6)". Suggestion: add one sentence noting that an earlier draft reported "zero" from a single seed — the ratchet illusion in miniature — which converts an embarrassment into the paper's best footnote.

R5 should · State the ratchet baseline convention (N1)

Add to Thm 5.1: "the first candidate is accepted unconditionally (empty incumbent); under a measured incumbent the count is Hₜ₊₁ − 1, an O(1) difference." One sentence.

R6 should · Repair the γ < 1 claim in §3 (N2)

Either add the exact-mass remark (E[T] = 1/(γq₀) when conditioning multiplies mass by exactly γ at every step) or weaken to "can slow search below cold sampling (e.g. for γ < ½ via Prop 3.3)".

R7 should · Harmonize N = 85 vs the constant 16 (N3)

Add a one-sided corollary: "against an incumbent of known mean, N ≥ (8σ²/Δ²) ln(B/δ) suffices" — then the main text can cite it honestly. Or simply use N = 170 in the figure; the green curve will look identical.

R8 should · Honest Fig 3 caption + Fig 2b inset (N4, W7, W9-iv)

Caption: distinguish "simulated algorithm" (first-error) from "exact law, plotted" (scalar, distance). Relabel the green dashed curve as "average case L/2+1". Fig 2b: log-y inset showing the gated rate ≈5×10⁻³/run rather than visually-exactly-zero.

R9 score-moving · Add one composed theorem (answers W1/Q2)

The "unifying theory" title currently rests on a definition. A theorem that needs the unified object is available from parts already proven. Sketch:

Theorem (certified-improvement run). Consider the loop of Def 2.1 with
proposer satisfying Assumption 3.1 (boost γ), σ-subgaussian evaluator,
and the gate of Prop 5.2 run at confidence δ' = δ/B with per-decision
budget N = ⌈(16σ²/Δ²) ln(B/δ)⌉. If every artifact in G_ε improves the
incumbent by ≥ Δ, then with probability ≥ 1 − 2δ the run makes no false
accept AND accepts an ε-good artifact within

    T ≤ ln(1/δ) / [ γ q₀(G_ε) (1 − δ/B) ]   proposals,

i.e. total evaluator calls ≤ N·T = O( (σ²/Δ²) ln(B/δ) ln(1/δ) / (γ q₀(G_ε)) ).

Proof: Thm 3.2(iii) gives the proposal tail; the gate accepts a true
Δ-improvement w.p. ≥ 1 − δ/B (Prop 5.2, other side of the same Hoeffding
bound); thin the geometric sequence accordingly; union-bound with Cor 5.3.

This couples prior mass, noise, and selection in one statement — the three resources the paper insists belong in one object. It is a rebuttal-scale addition (the pieces are all proven) and in my judgment moves the paper from 6 to 7.

R10 score-moving · Measure γ once (answers W2/Q1)

Rebuttal-scale protocol: take ~100 small buggy functions with unit tests; sample K = 50 patch proposals from one frozen open model per condition — (a) bug report only, (b) bug report + stack trace; report q̂(G) = pass-rate of proposals with Wilson intervals, and γ̂ = ratio. One GPU-day, and Assumption 3.1 stops being unfalsifiable. The blog companion's CUA/ShopGym harness experiment is the larger version of the same measurement.

Questions for the authors

Q1. Thm 5.1(ii): which acceptance convention do you intend — empty incumbent (Hₜ) or measured incumbent (Hₜ₊₁ − 1)? The rule as worded says the latter; the proof and code implement the former.

Q2. Can you state and prove the composed certified-improvement theorem (R9), or explain what obstruction prevents the geometric-thinning argument from going through when accepts feed back into the proposer's conditioning (the history-dependence Assumption 3.1 is supposed to absorb)?

Q3. For Prop 3.3 as used in §3: do you claim slowdown below cold sampling for all γ < 1, or only γ < ½? If the former, you need the exact-mass version (R6); please confirm which you will state.

Q4. The Occam bound's falsifiable prediction (validation size linear in artifact bits) is the most testable sentence in the paper. Do you have even one system's (ℓ(a), n, gap) triple — e.g. from GEPA's reported validation overfitting — to plot against it?

Verdict

6/10, Weak Accept, confidence 5/5. The dictionary (Def 2.1 + App I) is a real contribution the field needs and will cite; the proofs are correct as arguments; reproducibility hygiene is exemplary by theory-paper standards (seeded, vendored, bit-stable — verified). The defects are real but shallow: every one of E1–E4 and N1–N4 is fixable with the drop-ins above, none threatens a result's substance. What separates this from a 7–8 is structural: no theorem requires the unified object (W1), and the central adaptive assumption is never measured (W2). R9 + R10 are both rebuttal-scale and address exactly those two. I would raise to 7 on R1–R8 + R9, and consider 8 with a credible γ measurement.

I also note agreement with Reviewer 1 on every overlapping finding, established here by independent re-derivation and re-execution rather than by reading their review first — the two reports' numeric agreement (3.39σ, 8.18/8.30, 2 false accepts, ratio-to-bound ≈4) should give the AC high confidence in the factual record.
